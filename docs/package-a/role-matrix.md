## 2. Principals as implemented

**Verified**: `packages/contracts/src/principal.ts`, `apps/api/src/kernel/auth/auth.guard.ts`, `apps/api/src/kernel/authorization/authorization.service.ts`, `apps/api/src/modules/identity/services/principal-resolver.service.ts`, `apps/api/src/modules/identity/identity.module.ts`; tests `apps/api/src/kernel/__tests__/authorization.spec.ts`, `auth-and-operations.spec.ts`, `lazy-port.spec.ts`, `apps/api/src/modules/identity/__tests__/principal-resolver.spec.ts`, `apps/api/src/modules/identity/services/principal-resolver.service.spec.ts`.

Every request carries exactly one `Principal`. The identity module's `PrincipalResolver` is registered as `PRINCIPAL_RESOLVER_IMPL` (`identity.module.ts`); `AuthGuard` asks it for the principal of every verified bearer token and never reads the organization from the request (§4.8).

Which implementation answers each port (**Verified**):

- `PRINCIPAL_RESOLVER_IMPL` and `RELATIONSHIP_AUTHORITY_IMPL` are provided by `IdentityModule` as `useExisting` providers (`identity.module.ts`), `TEAM_AUTHORITY_IMPL` by `CompetitionModule` (`competition.module.ts`). The kernel resolves each token on first use through `lazyPort` (`lazy-port.ts`; test "resolves a useClass/useExisting implementation at call time, after container instantiation"). The assembled application (`apps/api/src/app.module.ts`) imports every module in `DOMAIN_MODULES` (`apps/api/src/modules/index.ts`, identity first), so all three implementations are present in the running API.
- When a module is not loaded, `kernel.module.ts` falls back with one logged warning: no principal for any token; `denyAllRelationshipAuthority`, which answers true only when actor and subject are the same person; `denyAllTeamAuthority`, which always answers false (`authorization.port.ts`). This happens in tests that keep the default `principalResolver: 'fake'` and import no module (`apps/api/src/testing/test-app.ts`, `FakePrincipalResolver`); the identity tests pass `principalResolver: 'module'` and run through the real resolver and relationship authority (`relationships.spec.ts`, `beforeAll`). See §8 item 6.
- `GET /v1/me` returns the principal, the person, the staff memberships and the permissions the person currently holds over each subject (`people.controller.ts`, `RelationshipAuthority.authoritiesOf`; test "GET /v1/me reports the principal, person, memberships and current authorities"). It carries no action: any authenticated principal reads their own record.

The enum has eleven values. The three remaining §2.1 roles (adult player, parent or guardian, team captain) are deliberately not staff roles: they are customer principals whose authority is a relationship, a grant or a team role (§5). A person may hold several enum values at once (`staffRoles` is an array; a membership row carries one or more roles). No role implies another: there is no role hierarchy in the kernel, and `hasStaffRole(principal, ...roles)` is a plain membership test.

## 6. A person may hold several roles; access depends on the action and the scope

§2.1: "A person may hold several roles. Their access depends on the specific action and scope, not merely a role label." The kernel evaluates one action against one target for one principal, in this order (**Verified**, `AuthorizationService.decide`):

1. Unknown action: deny.
2. Anonymous principal: deny (`AUTHENTICATION_REQUIRED`).
3. Organization mismatch: deny, for every kind.
4. System principal: allow.
5. Staff principal: if any of the action's `staffRoles` is held and the facility scope matches, allow (basis `staff role`). If a listed role is held but the facility does not match, deny (`facility scope mismatch`). If no listed role is held, continue.
6. Principal without a `personId`: skip to step 9. Otherwise, `allowSelf` and every subject is the principal's own person: if the action names no `subjectPermission`, allow (basis `self`); if it does, ask `RelationshipAuthorityPort.hasPermission(org, personId, personId, permission)` and allow (basis `self`) when it agrees, otherwise continue.
7. `subjectPermission` and the principal holds that grant for every subject (the port answers a subject equal to the principal exactly as in step 6): allow (basis `relationship:<permission>`).
8. `teamRole` and the target names a `teamEntryId` on which the principal holds that role per `TeamAuthorityPort`: allow (basis `team_role:<role>`).
9. Otherwise deny (`no applicable authority`), rendered as `PARTICIPANT_AUTHORITY_REQUIRED` when the target named subjects, `NOT_FOUND` when the target is protected, `ACCESS_DENIED` otherwise.

## 8. Known gaps and ambiguities

1. **Assignment-level scope for coaches and officials.** SPK-UX-007 and SPK-STAFF-004 scope a coach or official to assigned activities with time-bounded access. The kernel's staff path grants by role and facility only, and the registries already list `coach` and `official` facility-wide on reads such as `operations.read`, `scheduling.read_inventory`, `scheduling.read_schedule`, `scheduling.read_event`, `facilities.report_issue`, `competition.read_competitions` and `competition.read_fixture`, and `coach` on `competition.list_team_entries` and `competition.read_team_entry` (**Verified**; the full list is `permission-matrix.md` Ambiguities item 1). Each workforce, entry, competition, facilities and communications action that a coach or official may perform must load the assignment and compare its person with `principal.personId` before calling `authorization.require`, or the kernel must gain an assignment-scope target field. Such rows are marked ◐ in `permission-matrix.md`. **Proposed**.
2. **Facility mismatch stops guardian evaluation.** In `decide`, a staff principal who holds a listed role but is scoped to a different facility is denied at the staff step; the self, relationship and team paths are never reached. A coach scoped to facility A who is also the parent of a child active at facility B is denied `scheduling.read_event` on that child's event whenever the target names facility B, because `coach` is in that action's `staffRoles`. **Verified** behaviour (`authorization.service.ts`, the `roleOk && !facilityOk` branch); not covered by a test (`authorization.spec.ts` tests facility mismatch on a target without subjects). **Proposed** change: fall through to the customer paths on facility mismatch.
3. **One team role per action.** `ActionDefinition.teamRole` is a single value (**Verified** limitation). The competition module answers it with a downward hierarchy inside `TeamAuthority` (captain satisfies co-captain and member; co-captain satisfies member, and captain only under the rule-set flag), so captain-or-co-captain actions need no second registration (**Verified**). What the flag should be is **Pending decision** (owner: League manager; §7 item 4).
4. **No "delegate" grant.** The six SPK-IAM-003 permissions do not include the authority to grant or revoke permissions to another adult. The implementation uses `view` plus the service rule "only what you hold" for `identity.create_invitation`, staff roles for `identity.grant_access`, and the creator-guardian rule for self-service and revocation (**Verified**, §5.2). Whether a grantor should need an explicit delegation permission instead is **Pending decision** (owner: Operator/privacy owner; §7 item 1).
5. **RSVP permission.** SPK-IAM-003 has no permission for recording a child's availability. The registry maps `competition.respond_availability` to `register` (**Verified** registry, `competition.actions.ts`); the alternative is a seventh grant. **Pending decision** (owner: Product and operations; `decision-register.md` DEC-006 and DEC-064).
6. **Deny-all fallbacks apply only where the modules are absent.** `kernel.module.ts` still falls back to no principal, `denyAllRelationshipAuthority` and `denyAllTeamAuthority` when no module registers an implementation. In the running API every implementation is registered (§2), so the fallbacks are reached only in tests that keep `principalResolver: 'fake'` and import neither `IdentityModule` nor `CompetitionModule`. Two consequences are **Verified** by code reading: under the fallback a self check for `pay` or `collect` passes for any person because the fallback does not know who is a child (`actorPersonId === subjectPersonId`), and every guardian and team check answers false. The kernel's own tests avoid this by using fixture ports that mirror the identity contract (`authorization.spec.ts`, `service()`); `permission-matrix.md` Ambiguities item 14 records which module tests still run under the fallback. **Proposed**: every API test that exercises a `subjectPermission` or `teamRole` path imports the owning module with `principalResolver: 'module'`.
7. **Staff self-service.** The self path works for any principal with a `personId`, including staff (**Verified** by code: the staff branch continues to the self and relationship checks when no listed role matches). It is exercised end to end in the identity test "a coach who is also a parent": `GET /v1/me` reads the coach's own person through `identity.read_person`, and `coach` is not in `READ_STAFF`, so the read passes through the self path. Staff self-service rows in `permission-matrix.md` (own availability, accepting an assignment, submitting a fee) rely on this. A kernel test that names a staff principal reaching the self path does not exist yet. **Proposed**: add one.
8. **The identity README and two companion documents describe an older kernel.** `apps/api/src/modules/identity/README.md` says the `*_IMPL` registrations are `useValue` handles bound in `onModuleInit` and, under "Kernel limitations worked around here", that the kernel's self short-circuit lets a child act on themselves for `pay` and `collect` and that the relationship port is called without a transaction handle. `identity.module.ts` registers both with `useExisting` and has no `onModuleInit`; `authorization.service.ts` asks the port for every self decision that names a permission; `apps/api/src/kernel/database/transaction-context.ts` carries the active transaction and `identity.transaction-context.ts` is now a thin alias over it. `docs/architecture/overview.md` ("Identity and authorization") repeats the `useValue` and transaction-handle limitations, and `permission-matrix.md` (Part A, A.1 and A.4 step 8, Ambiguities item 10) still says no module registers `TEAM_AUTHORITY_IMPL` and lists competition among the modules without an actions file; `competition.module.ts` registers it and `competition.actions.ts` declares 18 actions. **Verified** discrepancies; none of those files is this document's to change.
9. **Revocation beyond identity.** SPK-IAM-006 requires revocation to affect exports, private feed retrieval and protected messages awaiting dispatch. Identity publishes `identity.access_grant_revoked`, `identity.access_restricted` and `identity.access_restriction_lifted` (**Verified**), but no module subscribes yet because communications and reporting are scaffolds. The handlers, idempotent under replay per `CLAUDE.md`, are **Proposed**.
10. **Restrictions and expiries need a sweeper.** A `restrict` decision recorded with an `effectiveTo` is not lifted automatically, and pending invitations and claims are marked expired only when someone attempts to accept them (identity `README.md`, "Known gaps"; `invitations.service.ts`). The authority checks are unaffected because they evaluate dates at the clock, but the stored statuses lag. **Proposed**: a scheduled job that records `lift` decisions and expiry transitions through the same state machines.
11. **Verified contact points and claims.** A guardian claim for a child and an invitation by contact point require the acceptor's account to be linked to a person who holds that verified contact (test "staff invitations by contact point require the acceptor to hold that verified contact"), and only staff may mark a contact point verified (test "SPK-IAM-001: an adult customer may create a child record (self-service) but not an adult, and cannot mark contacts verified"). How a contact is verified in production depends on the identity and messaging providers, which are not selected (§5.2). **Pending decision** (owner: Technical lead/account owners).
